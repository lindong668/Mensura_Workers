How to Separate Baby Spider Plants: Propagation Guide

Published 2026-09-28 09:52 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

A spider plant is carrying several small plantlets on arching runners and you want to root them without losing them. Root the plantlet while still attached, or cut it once it has small nubs and keep it barely moist until established. Give each step time to show its effect before adding another intervention, otherwise you cannot tell which one helped.

What You’ll Need

For spider plant plantlets, the useful kit is short and ordinary:

  • Clean snips — for a clean cut that does not tear plant tissue
  • A light free-draining mix — to keep the root zone airy and free-draining
  • A jar of water (optional) — for a thorough, even watering without over-soaking

Safety note. Keep snips and small pots out of reach of pets; spider plant is non-toxic but should not be chewed. Work on a stable surface, and keep pets and children away from cut material.

They separate a simple spider plant plantlets problem from one that needs a different response entirely:

  • The plantlet has several leaves and small pale root nubs at its base.
  • It is at least a few centimetres across.
  • The parent is healthy and actively producing runners.

Step-by-Step Guide

Work through the steps in order; each one builds on the previous, and you can stop once the plant is recovering.

  1. Step 1 — Choose a plantlet that already shows small pale root nubs at the base

    Those nubs are pre-formed roots; a very tiny bare plantlet takes far longer and fails more often.

  2. Step 2 — Either pin the plantlet into a small pot of mix while still attached to the parent, or cut the runner close to it

    Rooting while attached is the lowest-risk method because the parent keeps supplying water.

  3. Step 3 — If you cut it, place the base just at the surface of a lightly moist mix, not buried deep

    The small root nubs need contact with the medium but also air.

  4. Step 4 — Keep it in bright indirect light and the mix barely moist for the first couple of weeks

    A new plantlet has little reserve and no established root system yet.

  5. Step 5 — Cut the connecting runner only after you feel resistance when you gently tug the plantlet

    Resistance means its own roots have anchored into the mix.

  6. Step 6 — Once established, move it to a normal watering routine and watch for new centre growth

    That is the signal it has become an independent plant.

Why This Works

Chlorophytum produces plantlets on stolons, and each one develops small adventitious root initials at its base before it is fully independent. Rooting it while still attached exploits the parent's water supply during that vulnerable interval, which is why it fails less often than cutting first. Once those initials elongate into the medium, the plantlet can support itself and the connecting stolon is no longer needed.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on spider plant plantlets: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it.

What to Avoid

These are the common mistakes that set the plant back; avoid them while the problem persists.

  • Do not cut off a very small plantlet with no root nubs.
  • Do not bury the base deep in wet mix.
  • Do not let a detached plantlet dry out completely.
  • Do not put it in direct sun while rooting.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep spider plant plantlets from drifting back into the same situation.

  • Keep the parent watered normally while a plantlet is attached.
  • Label the date if you root several.
  • Use a small pot; a large one stays wet around a tiny root system.
  • Expect a couple of weeks before the runner can be cut.

FAQs

When is a spider plantlet ready?

When it has several leaves and small pale root nubs at the base, usually a few centimetres across.

Should I root it in water first?

You can, but pinning it into mix while still attached is simpler and avoids a second transition.

How do I know it has rooted?

Gently tug: resistance means its own roots have anchored. Then cut the runner.

Why did my plantlet shrivel?

It was too small, dried out, or sat in a large wet pot with no established roots.

Final Thoughts

Pick a plantlet with root nubs, root it attached in lightly moist mix, then cut the runner once it resists a gentle tug.

Where this guide applies

This applies to a plant in ordinary indoor potting mix.
