Care Basics

Watering, potting soil, humidity, light levels, fertiliser, pot size, drainage, leaf cleaning, temperature and acclimation.

Each guide below is written around one concrete problem: what you can see on the plant, what is happening in the pot, and what to do next. Every page gives the full answer in its initial HTML, with a materials list, ordered steps, mistakes to avoid, routine tips and short FAQs.

Guide 1 · 7 sections

How Often Should You Water Indoor Plants? General Rule

Water when the mix has dried to the depth the plant needs — not on a calendar. Species, pot size, light and season set the interval.

Guide 2 · 7 sections

Best Potting Soil for Indoor Plants: Complete Buying Guide

Match the mix structure to the plant: airy bark for aroids, gritty mineral for succulents, fine but free-draining for thin-rooted plants.

Guide 3 · 7 sections

How to Increase Humidity for Houseplants: 10 Easy Ways

Stable local humidity helps; a pebble tray, grouping, a humidifier and moving plants away from heaters do more than repeated misting.

Guide 4 · 7 sections

What Is Indirect Light for Plants? How to Tell Light Levels

Bright indirect light means a spot close to a bright window but out of the direct sunbeam — bright enough to read comfortably all day.

Guide 5 · 7 sections

Best Natural Fertilizers for Houseplants: DIY Options

Use mild, diluted, well-decomposed options sparingly; in small indoor pots the main risk is always over-application.

Guide 6 · 7 sections

How to Choose the Right Pot Size for Your Plant

Choose a pot only slightly larger than the current root ball; excess mix volume is a bigger risk than a snug fit.

Guide 7 · 7 sections

Do Indoor Plants Need Drainage Holes? What to Know

Yes — for almost all indoor plants, water must be able to leave the pot. Use a cachepot with an inner draining pot instead.

Guide 8 · 7 sections

How to Clean Plant Leaves: Safe Methods for All Plants

Wipe smooth leaves with a damp cloth; never wipe waxy, fuzzy or powdered foliage, and support the leaf with your other hand.

Guide 9 · 7 sections

Best Temperatures for Houseplants: Winter and Summer

Most tropical houseplants are comfortable in the same range people are; the real enemies are cold glass, draughts and hot dry heater airflow.

Guide 10 · 7 sections

How to Acclimate New Plants to Your Home: Step by Step

Quarantine, keep conditions stable, and let light and watering adapt gradually over two to three weeks.

Guide 11 · 7 sections

Do Houseplants Need a Fixed Watering Schedule? Or Check the Soil

Most houseplants should be watered by soil dryness, not a calendar. Light, season, pot size and plant type all change how fast the mix dries.
