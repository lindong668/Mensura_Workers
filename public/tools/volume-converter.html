Volume Converter

Convert across 15 volume units including both gallon standards, both fluid ounce standards, and the three different cup measures used in recipes around the world.

Two gallons, a 20% difference

The most common volume error. The US gallon is 3.785411784 L. The Imperial (UK) gallon is 4.54609 L — about 20% larger. The same applies to their subdivisions: a US pint is 473 mL while an Imperial pint is 568 mL, and ordering "a pint" in London gets you noticeably more beer than in New York.

The US gallon is legally defined as 231 cubic inches, a specification inherited from the pre-1824 wine gallon. The Imperial gallon was redefined in 1824 as the volume of 10 pounds of water at a specific temperature, then fixed at 4.54609 L in 1985.

Three cups, three different amounts

A "cup" in a recipe is not a universal measure. The US legal cup is 240 mL, the Japanese cup is 200 mL, and the Australian cup is 250 mL. A recipe developed with one and baked with another is off by up to 20% — enough to ruin a cake. The cooking converter carries these cup standards alongside per-ingredient conversions.

Dry pints and liquid pints are not the same pint

The United States keeps two parallel sets of volume units, and the word "pint" alone does not tell you which one applies. A US liquid pint is 473.176473 mL. A US dry pint is 550.6104713575 mL — about 16% larger. The same split runs through quarts: 946.35 mL liquid against 1101.22 mL dry.

Liquid measures are what recipes, drinks and fuel use. Dry measures apply to berries, grains and other goods sold by volume rather than weight. A US dry barrel and peck belong to the dry set as well. In practice almost everything you meet outside agriculture is the liquid set, but a conversion that assumes the wrong one is off by a sixth — far too much to dismiss.

Why the numbers never divide cleanly

Metric volume is decimal and cubic — 1 mL is 1 cm³, 1000 L is 1 m³. Imperial volume descends from successive halving: gallon → quart → pint → cup → fluid ounce, with 16 cups and 128 fluid ounces to a US gallon. The two systems are structurally incompatible, which is why exact factors look arbitrary.
