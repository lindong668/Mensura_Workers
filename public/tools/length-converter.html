Length Converter

Convert between 11 length units spanning nine orders of magnitude — from nanometers to nautical miles. All conversions use exact international definitions, so results are reproducible rather than rounded.

The one definition that fixes everything

Since the 1959 international yard-and-pound agreement, the inch is defined as exactly 25.4 millimetres. Every other imperial length unit derives from it: a foot is exactly 0.3048 m, a yard 0.9144 m, and a mile 1609.344 m. Because these are exact by definition, converting between metric and imperial never introduces rounding error — any discrepancy you see comes from the number of decimal places displayed, not from the conversion itself.

Survey feet are not international feet

The trap most converters miss. The United States maintains two different feet. The international foot (0.3048 m) is used for everyday and most engineering work. The US survey foot (1200/3937 m ≈ 0.3048006 m) is still used in some US state plane coordinate systems and older land-survey data. The difference is only about 2 parts per million, but over a 100 km survey line that is roughly 20 cm — enough to matter in legal boundary work.

This tool uses the international foot. If you are working with legacy US geospatial data, confirm which foot your source uses before mixing coordinates.

Nautical miles measure something different

A statute mile (1609.344 m) is an arbitrary historical length. A nautical mile (1852 m, exact) is derived from the Earth: it was originally one minute of latitude. That is why navigation and aviation use nautical miles and knots — a distance in nautical miles maps directly onto a chart's latitude scale.

Choosing sensible precision

Converting 1 metre gives 39.3700787… inches, but reporting nine decimals implies a measurement accuracy nobody has. A practical rule: keep roughly one more significant figure than your input justified. If you measured to the nearest centimetre, metres-to-feet at two decimal places is already more than honest.
