How Often to Water a Monstera Deliciosa (Complete Guide)

Published 2026-09-23 07:23 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

You keep reading "water once a week", but your monstera lives in a different room, a different pot and a different season than whoever wrote that. Water a monstera when the top five to eight centimetres of mix are dry, not on a schedule. In most homes that lands somewhere between seven and fourteen days. Work in this order; each step depends on the one before it, and skipping ahead usually means undoing it later.

What You’ll Need

For monstera deliciosa, the useful kit is short and ordinary:

  • A long wooden skewer or chopstick — to judge the plant’s condition before you act
  • A saucer or tray — to give the plant the right spot and conditions while it recovers
  • A pot with unobstructed drainage — so water can drain freely and never sit under the roots
  • A note on your phone to record dates for the first month — to judge the plant’s condition before you act

Safety note. Lift large pots with care; a wet 30 cm pot is heavy. Water away from electrical sockets. Work on a stable surface, and keep pets and children away from cut material.

They separate a simple monstera deliciosa problem from one that needs a different response entirely:

  • A wooden skewer comes out clean and dry from the upper half of the pot.
  • The pot feels noticeably lighter when you lift it.
  • The newest leaf is still unfurling normally and the petioles are firm.

Step-by-Step Guide

Work through the steps in order; each one builds on the previous, and you can stop once the plant is recovering.

  1. Step 1 — Push a skewer deep into the mix, wait, and read the damp line

    The surface dries days before the root ball does, so judging by touch alone systematically over- or under-waters a large pot.

  2. Step 2 — When the top five to eight centimetres are dry, pour slowly over the whole surface until water runs from the drainage hole

    A partial splash wets only one side of the root ball and leaves dry pockets that later show as brown leaf edges.

  3. Step 3 — Let the pot drain completely for at least fifteen minutes before returning it to its saucer

    Standing water re-wets the base and undoes the drying cycle you just created.

  4. Step 4 — Record the date and the number of days since the last watering

    Two or three cycles give you a real interval for your room, which is more useful than any generic weekly rule.

  5. Step 5 — Recheck the interval when the heating goes on or the plant pushes a new leaf

    A monstera in active growth uses noticeably more water than the same plant in a cool, dim winter.

  6. Step 6 — Inspect the lower leaves for yellowing at the next cycle

    Soft yellow lower leaves mean the interval is still too short, even if the schedule looks reasonable.

Why This Works

Monstera is a hemiepiphyte: in nature it climbs with roots that grip bark and are exposed to air between rains. A dense, permanently saturated mix is the opposite of that environment. The plant tolerates a dry-down between soakings because its thick petioles and stems hold reserves, but it cannot tolerate roots sitting in oxygen-free water for days.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on monstera deliciosa: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it.

What to Avoid

Each item below reverses one of the steps you just read.

  • Do not give a little water every day to "keep it evenly moist".
  • Do not let the pot sit in a decorative cachepot full of runoff.
  • Do not assume a bigger pot needs more frequent watering.
  • Do not mist instead of watering the root ball.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep monstera deliciosa from drifting back into the same situation.

  • Use room-temperature water to avoid shocking a warm root ball.
  • Water in the morning so the surface can dry before night.
  • Aerate a compacted mix gently with a skewer, never by digging near the stem.
  • Treat the aroid mix as part of the answer: coarse bark and perlite make the interval forgiving.

FAQs

Is once a week right for monstera?

It is a starting guess, not a rule. Pot size, mix, light and season can double or halve the real interval.

Should I mist my monstera instead?

No. Misting raises humidity around the leaves for minutes and does nothing for the root ball; it is not a substitute for watering.

Why are the lower leaves yellow after I watered?

The interval was too short or the pot did not drain. Check the skewer before the next watering rather than adding feed.

Does a moss pole change how often I water?

Yes. A damp pole adds moisture and usually lengthens the interval slightly, so keep using the skewer.

Final Thoughts

Replace the calendar with a skewer test, then let your own two or three cycles set the interval.

Where this guide applies

This applies to a plant in ordinary indoor potting mix.
