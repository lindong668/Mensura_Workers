Date Duration Calculator

Find the number of days between two dates, with weeks, hours and approximate years shown alongside. Leap years are handled automatically by using real calendar arithmetic rather than a fixed 365-day year.

This counts the difference between dates (end − start). Monday to Wednesday is 2 days. Add one if you need inclusive counting.

Counting days versus counting the gap

An off-by-one that catches everyone. Between Monday and Wednesday there is a 2-day difference, but the period spans 3 days if you count Monday itself. This tool reports the difference in days (end minus start), which is what contracts and deadlines usually mean. If you need inclusive counting, add one day.

Leap years

A calendar year is not 365 days — it averages 365.2425 days under the Gregorian rules: leap every 4 years, except centuries, except centuries divisible by 400. So 2000 was a leap year, 1900 was not, and 2100 will not be. Using a fixed 365-day year drifts by roughly one day every four years, which is why this tool converts using real calendar dates.

Months are not a fixed length

"How many months?" depends on which months. February to March is 28 or 29 days; July to August is 31. That is why the tool reports days and weeks exactly, and years only as an approximation based on 365.2425 days. For legal or financial periods, specify days rather than months.

Common uses

  • Contract and notice periods — where "within 30 days" means calendar days.
  • Project elapsed time — distinguishing working calendars from raw day counts.
  • Deadlines and cut-offs — where "within 30 days" means calendar days, not working days.

Why business days are hard to count

Many deadlines are written in working days, and this tool counts calendar days. The gap between the two is not a fixed ratio. A week contains five working days, but public holidays shift the count per country and per year, and some jurisdictions count Saturday as a working day while others do not.

Because of that, no single formula converts calendar days to business days reliably. The practical approach is to count calendar days here, then subtract weekends and the specific holidays your contract or jurisdiction recognises — which is why legal documents usually name the calendar rather than leave it implied.
