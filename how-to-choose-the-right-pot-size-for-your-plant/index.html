How to Choose the Right Pot Size for Your Plant

Published 2026-10-14 15:38 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

You are unsure whether to repot one size up or give a plant a generous new container "so it can grow". Choose a pot only slightly larger than the current root ball; excess mix volume is a bigger risk than a snug fit. Work in this order; each step depends on the one before it, and skipping ahead usually means undoing it later.

Why This Works

A potting mix holds water in proportion to its volume, but only the roots that occupy it remove that water. If the container is far larger than the root ball, the outer zone stays saturated long after the inner zone has dried, losing air-filled pores and inviting rot. Conversely, a very snug pot dries fast and may need more frequent watering. The right size is the smallest volume that still gives the roots room for the next season of growth.

What to Avoid

The list is short because Do not jump three sizes up and its close variants cover most relapses. Each item below reverses one of the steps you just read.

  • Do not jump three sizes up.
  • Do not put a small plant in a deep heavy container for looks.
  • Do not bury the stem deeper to steady the plant.
  • Do not repot a struggling plant before checking for root rot.

  • Use a saucer you actually empty.
  • Match pot material to your watering habits; unglazed clay dries faster.
  • Recheck the watering interval for the first month.
  • Use the plant's stability as a check on whether the pot is proportionate.

FAQs

Is a bigger pot better?

No. A large volume of wet mix around a small root ball is one of the most common causes of root rot.

How much bigger?

Typically two to four centimetres wider — one nominal pot size up.

Should I repot every spring?

Only when the roots or the mix give you a reason. Many plants prefer to stay snug.

Why is my plant wilting after repotting?

Often the new mix is being watered on the old schedule, or the crown was buried.

Final Thoughts

One size up, real drainage, same depth, then relearn the watering interval in the new volume.
