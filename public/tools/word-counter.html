Word & Character Counter

Count words, characters, bytes, sentences, lines and paragraphs in any text, with an estimated reading time. Runs entirely in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

Characters are not bytes

The distinction that breaks SMS billing and database limits. A Latin letter is 1 byte in UTF-8, but an accented character like "é" is 2 bytes, a CJK character like "中" is 3, and an emoji is typically 4. A 100-character message that looks short can be 300 bytes. Character limits and byte limits are different constraints, and only one of them applies to your situation.

What counts as a word?

There is no universal definition. The common approach — and the one used here — splits on whitespace, which means hyphenated and punctuation-attached tokens behave differently across tools. "state-of-the-art" is one word by whitespace splitting, three by punctuation splitting. For contractual or editorial word counts, confirm which convention the other party uses.

Reading time estimates

The 200 words-per-minute figure is a long-standing average for silent reading of English prose. Technical material, dense legal text and second-language reading all run slower. Treat it as a rough magnitude, not a specification.

Where byte counts matter

  • SMS — segment limits are defined in bytes under GSM or UTF-8 encoding, so a message of 160 characters may split into multiple segments.
  • Database columns — VARCHAR(n) may count characters or bytes depending on the engine and collation.
  • API payload limits — usually specified in bytes, so emoji-heavy content hits the ceiling far sooner than plain text.
