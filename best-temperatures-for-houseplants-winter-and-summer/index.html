Best Temperatures for Houseplants: Winter and Summer

Published 2026-10-11 07:31 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

A plant near a window declines every winter and you suspect temperature rather than water. Most tropical houseplants are comfortable in the same range people are; the real enemies are cold glass, draughts and hot dry heater airflow. Start with the simplest check, because the most obvious-looking cause is often not the one at work.

Why This Works

Tropical foliage has little cold tolerance because its membranes are adapted to a narrow, stable temperature range; chilling causes leakage and cell death that shows as dark softened tissue. At the other end, warm dry moving air steepens the leaf-to-air vapour gradient, so water loss outruns root uptake. Between those two, ordinary room temperature is fine — the failures are almost always local extremes at the windowsill or the heater, not the thermometer in the middle of the room.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on houseplant temperature: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it. Diagnose first, act once, then leave A cheap room thermometer — alone long enough to answer.

What to Avoid

These are the shortcuts that usually follow a diagnosis of Best Temperatures for Houseplants: Winter and. The list is short because Do not let leaves press against cold and its close variants cover most relapses.

  • Do not let leaves press against cold window glass.
  • Do not place a tropical plant directly over a heater.
  • Do not keep watering on a summer schedule in winter.
  • Do not assume a warm room guarantees a warm windowsill.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep houseplant temperature from drifting back into the same situation. Fold them into the watering round so A cheap room thermometer — gets checked at the same time.

  • Use a thermometer at the actual plant position, not across the room.
  • Watch the first cold night of the season closely.
  • Group plants away from exterior doors in winter.
  • Recheck the sun path and the glass temperature when the season turns.

FAQs

What temperature do houseplants prefer?

Most tropical foliage plants are happy in the high teens to mid twenties Celsius — ordinary indoor comfort.

Why does my plant get black spots in winter?

Often cold glass or a cold draught. Chilling injury appears the next morning as soft dark tissue.

Is a radiator bad?

Yes, if the plant sits in the hot dry airflow. Move it a metre or two away.

Should I water less in winter?

Usually yes. Slower growth and lower light mean much lower water use.

Final Thoughts

Stable ordinary room temperature, off cold glass and out of heater airflow, with less water in winter.
