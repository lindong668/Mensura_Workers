Do Indoor Plants Need Drainage Holes? What to Know

Published 2026-10-18 20:26 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

You like a sealed decorative pot but have lost plants in them before. Yes — for almost all indoor plants, water must be able to leave the pot. Use a cachepot with an inner draining pot instead. Why This Works

Water leaves a container through drainage holes because gravity overcomes the capillary forces holding it in the mix. In a sealed vessel it cannot leave, so a saturated zone persists at the base — exactly where the oldest roots sit. A gravel layer does not create an exit; it changes the height of the perched water table, often bringing it nearer the root crown. The cachepot arrangement keeps the appearance while preserving the physics.

What to Avoid

Four responses undo the work above; Do not plant directly into a container is the one people reach for first. Avoid these while Water exits freely within seconds of a thorough soak is still present, not only after it clears.

  • Do not plant directly into a container with no drainage.
  • Do not assume gravel at the bottom replaces a hole.
  • Do not leave the inner pot standing in collected runoff.
  • Do not water a cachepot on the same schedule as a freely draining pot.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep drainage holes from drifting back into the same situation. After the repair, Use a spacer or upturned saucer is the habit most worth keeping.

  • Use a spacer or upturned saucer to keep the inner pot above any stray water.
  • Empty the outer pot whenever you water.
  • Check the drainage holes yearly for salt and root blockage.
  • Group cachepots with plants that prefer the same drying speed.

FAQs

Can a plant survive without drainage holes?

With very careful watering, briefly. As a long-term setup it is the most reliable route to root rot.

Does gravel at the bottom work?

No. It does not provide an exit and can actually hold the wet zone closer to the roots.

What is a cachepot for?

It is a decorative outer sleeve. Keep the plant in a draining inner pot and lift it out to water.

Why does the lower leaf yellow first?

The lowest roots sit in the accumulated water and lose oxygen first.

Final Thoughts

Use a draining inner pot inside the decorative one, drain fully before returning it, and never trust a gravel layer.
