Why This Works

Monstera, like other aroids, grows from axillary buds located at stem nodes — the same buds that produce leaves, aerial roots and lateral shoots on a mature vine. A detached leaf blade and petiole contain no such bud, so they can photosynthesise for a while but cannot generate a new plant. Once a node is included, success depends on keeping that bud moist while the cut stem end stays oxygenated, which is why an airy medium beats stagnant water for many growers.

FAQs

Why will my monstera cutting not grow?

Almost always it has no node. A leaf and petiole alone have no meristem to produce a new vine.

What does a node look like?

A slightly swollen ring or joint on the stem, often with a small brown bump or a short aerial root.

Can I root it in soil directly?

Yes, in a loose moist aroid mix or sphagnum, as long as the node stays in contact with the medium.

How long until roots appear?

Usually two to six weeks depending on warmth and light; a new leaf comes later.

Final Thoughts

Cut below a node, keep the bud moist but aerated, and wait for branched roots before potting into an airy mix.
