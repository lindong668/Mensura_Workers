How to Care for Chinese Evergreen (Aglaonema) Indoors: Beginner Guide

Published 2026-11-24 10:00 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

A Chinese evergreen sits in a dim corner, holds up for weeks without attention, and then suddenly drops older leaves or develops brown edges. Aglaonema is one of the most forgiving foliage houseplants, but it fails in the same two situations again and again: cold air touching it, and wet soil that never dries. This guide covers light, watering, feeding and routine care so the plant stays bushy through the year.

What You’ll Need

For this guide, the useful kit is short and ordinary:

  • A warm spot out of draughts — away from doors, windows that leak cold air, and air-conditioning vents.
  • A pot with a drainage hole and an emptyable saucer — aglaonema hates sitting in trapped water.
  • Standard free-draining potting mix — ordinary houseplant mix with perlite works well.
  • A clean, damp cloth for wiping leaves — dust blocks light and slows photosynthesis.
  • A liquid houseplant fertiliser at half strength — only needed during the growing months.

Safety note. Wear gloves if you are trimming rotted tissue, and keep tools away from children and pets. Work on a stable surface, and keep pets and children away from cut material.

These signs separate the two situations:

  • Lower leaves yellow and soften while the newest growth at the centre stays green.
  • Leaf edges turn dry and brown, especially on the side facing a cold window or door.
  • The mix feels cool and damp several days after watering rather than drying down.

Step-by-Step Guide

  1. Step 1 — Place it in bright to medium indirect light

    Aglaonema survives in low light, but variegated varieties lose their markings and grow slowly there.

  2. Step 2 — Keep the root zone warm, above 16 degrees Celsius

    These are tropical plants.

  3. Step 3 — Water when the top three to five centimetres are dry

    Push a finger into the mix before watering.

  4. Step 4 — Empty the saucer after each watering

    Water pooled in a decorative outer pot keeps the lower roots starved of oxygen.

  5. Step 5 — Feed lightly from spring to autumn

    Use an ordinary liquid houseplant feed diluted to half strength once a month in the growing season.

  6. Step 6 — Wipe the leaves and trim only fully damaged foliage

    Dust the broad leaves with a damp cloth so they can photosynthesise efficiently.

Why This Works

Aglaonema evolved as a forest-floor plant, used to filtered light and warm, humus-rich soil that drains between rains. Its thick stems hold moisture, which is why it forgives a missed watering but reacts badly to soggy, cold roots.

That history also explains the failure pattern: cold and wet arrive together in a draughty, over-watered spot, and the plant sheds its oldest leaves first to protect the growing centre. Keeping warmth and drainage separate from each other prevents most problems before they start.

What to Avoid

These are the common mistakes that set the plant back; avoid them while the problem persists.

  • Do not place it near a door, balcony gap or air-conditioning vent that blows cold air across the leaves.
  • Do not keep the mix perpetually moist; soggy roots are the most common reason aglaonema declines indoors.
  • Do not feed on a fixed schedule through winter, when the plant is hardly growing and salts accumulate.
  • Do not move a struggling plant into direct sun hoping it will dry out faster; this burns the foliage.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep the plant from drifting back into the same situation.

  • Rotate the pot a quarter turn each month so every side receives even light.
  • Check the newest central leaf when judging health, rather than the oldest lower leaf.
  • Keep humidity moderate; a pebble tray helps in a dry heated room, but misting alone will not fix wet soil.
  • Repot only when roots circle the pot, and choose a vessel one size larger, not much bigger.

FAQs

Can aglaonema grow in a dark corner?

It will survive there, but growth slows and variegation fades. For a bushy, colourful plant, give it medium indirect light instead.

Why are the tips turning brown?

Most often it is cold air, dry air around heating, or soil that stayed too wet. Check the temperature and moisture before reaching for fertiliser.

Is Chinese evergreen toxic to pets?

Aglaonema contains calcium oxalate crystals that irritate mouths and stomachs if chewed. Keep it out of reach of cats and dogs that nibble leaves.

How often should I water it?

Let the top three to five centimetres dry between waterings. In a typical home that is roughly once a week in warm months and less often in winter.

Final Thoughts

Keep it warm, bright-indirect and dry between drinks; aglaonema problems usually trace back to cold draughts or a perpetually wet root zone, not to thirst.

Where this guide applies

These steps assume a plastic or glazed pot with a working drainage hole.
