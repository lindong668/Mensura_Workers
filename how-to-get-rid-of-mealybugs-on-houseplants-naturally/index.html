How to Get Rid of Mealybugs on Houseplants Naturally

Published 2026-11-04 10:38 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

Small white cottony clusters sit in leaf axils and along stems, and the plant is getting sticky. Mealybugs hide in crevices and protect themselves with wax, so physical removal plus repeat treatment beats one spray. The sequence matters here: diagnose first, then act once, then leave the plant alone long enough to respond.

What You’ll Need

For mealybugs, the useful kit is short and ordinary:

  • Cotton swabs — to treat or wipe the affected parts carefully
  • Isopropyl alcohol at a dilute concentration — to treat or wipe the affected parts carefully
  • A soft cloth — to treat or wipe the affected parts carefully
  • A spray bottle with mild soapy water — to treat or wipe the affected parts carefully
  • A hand lens (optional) — to judge the plant’s condition before you act

Safety note. Keep alcohol away from flames and out of reach of children; test one leaf before treating a whole plant. Work on a stable surface, and keep pets and children away from cut material.

They separate a simple mealybugs problem from one that needs a different response entirely:

  • White fluffy masses in axils, under leaves and at the soil line.
  • Sticky honeydew on leaves or the surface below.
  • New growth emerging distorted or stunted.

Step-by-Step Guide

Work through the steps in order; each one builds on the previous, and you can stop once the plant is recovering.

  1. Step 1 — Isolate the affected plant immediately and check every neighbour in the same room

    Mealybugs crawl between touching pots, and a single untreated plant restarts the cycle.

  2. Step 2 — Dab each visible cluster with a swab dipped in dilute alcohol, working into axils and under leaf midribs

    Direct contact dissolves the waxy coating that makes most sprays ineffective.

  3. Step 3 — Wipe the whole stem and both leaf surfaces with a mild soapy cloth

    Crawlers and eggs are far smaller than the visible cottony adults and are easily missed.

  4. Step 4 — Check the pot rim, the drainage hole and the top layer of mix

    Mealybugs also feed on roots and crown tissue below the surface.

  5. Step 5 — Repeat the treatment every five to seven days for at least three rounds

    The wax protects eggs and crawlers, so one pass only removes the adults you can see.

  6. Step 6 — If root infestation is confirmed, repot into fresh mix after washing the roots

    Soil-dwelling populations survive any foliar treatment alone.

Why This Works

Mealybugs are sap-feeding hemipterans coated in a waxy filament that repels water-based sprays. They congregate in protected axils where airflow is low, excrete sugary honeydew and can transmit viruses or distort new growth. Their life cycle includes eggs and highly mobile crawlers, so any single treatment addresses only the visible adults. Mechanical removal plus repetition across the full cycle is what actually clears a collection.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on mealybugs: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it.

What to Avoid

These are the common mistakes that set the plant back; avoid them while the problem persists.

  • Do not blast the plant with a strong hose indoors.
  • Do not rely on a single spray as a cure.
  • Do not fertilise an infested plant to "help it recover".
  • Do not return it to the group until you have seen two clean weeks.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep mealybugs from drifting back into the same situation.

  • Inspect new plants before they join your collection.
  • Keep some space between pots so pests cannot walk across.
  • Quarantine any purchase for two weeks.
  • Check the newest growth first, which is where mealybugs prefer to feed.

FAQs

Are mealybugs the same as scale?

Related but different. Mealybugs are soft, mobile and cottony; scale forms hard, stationary shells.

Why do they keep coming back?

Eggs and crawlers survive the first treatment, and root-dwelling colonies are easy to miss. Repeat weekly.

Is alcohol safe for all plants?

Test one leaf first; thin or waxy foliage can be sensitive. Wipe rather than soak.

Do they spread to other plants?

Yes, by crawling between touching pots and on hands, tools and clothing. Isolate first.

Final Thoughts

Isolate, swab every crevice with dilute alcohol, repeat weekly for three rounds, and check the roots too.

Where this guide applies

These steps assume a plastic or glazed pot with a working drainage hole.
