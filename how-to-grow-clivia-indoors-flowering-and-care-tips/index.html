How to Grow Clivia Indoors: Flowering and Care Tips

Published 2026-11-10 08:23 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

A clivia has big healthy strap leaves but no flower stalk year after year. Clivia needs a cool, dry winter rest and bright indirect light to initiate a flower stalk. Start with the simplest check, because the most obvious-looking cause is often not the one at work.

Why This Works

Clivia miniata is a forest-floor plant from southern Africa with thick evergreen leaves and fleshy water-storing roots. In its native seasonal climate, a cooler drier winter precedes spring flowering, and that contrast is the cue for the apical meristem to switch from leaf to inflorescence. Because it stores reserves slowly, a young plant may need several seasons of leaf building before it has enough to hold a stalk.

What to Avoid

Each item below reverses one of the steps you just read. Four responses undo the work above; Do not repot annually into a bigger is the one people reach for first.

  • Do not repot annually into a bigger pot.
  • Do not water heavily through the winter rest.
  • Do not cut healthy leaves to tidy the fan.
  • Do not place it in direct midday sun.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep clivia from drifting back into the same situation. These small routines stop How to Grow Clivia Indoors: Flowering coming back without adding products.

  • Mark the start of the rest period so it is repeatable.
  • Keep it away from radiator heat during the cool phase.
  • Watch the centre of the fan, where the stalk appears.
  • Be patient: a young clivia needs several years of leaf growth before it flowers.

FAQs

Why will my clivia not flower?

Most often it never got a cool, drier winter rest, or it is too young and too small.

How long is the rest period?

About six to eight weeks of cooler conditions and noticeably reduced watering.

Should I repot it to encourage blooming?

Usually no. Clivia flowers better slightly snug; repot only when the pot is truly failing.

Can I force it with fertiliser?

No. Feed supports growth, but the seasonal temperature and moisture shift is the trigger.

Final Thoughts

Bright indirect light, a cool drier winter rest, a snug pot, and patience for a young plant.
