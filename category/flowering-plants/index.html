Flowering Indoor Plants

Orchids, kalanchoe, anthurium, African violets, jasmine, begonias, clivia and bromeliads — getting them to bloom and keeping the flowers.

Each guide below is written around one concrete problem: what you can see on the plant, what is happening in the pot, and what to do next. Every page gives the full answer in its initial HTML, with a materials list, ordered steps, mistakes to avoid, routine tips and short FAQs.

Guide 1 · 7 sections

How to Get an Orchid to Rebloom: Step-by-Step Guide

Reblooming is triggered by a sustained modest temperature drop plus bright indirect light; feeding is secondary.

Guide 2 · 7 sections

How to Care for Kalanchoe: Keep It Blooming Longer

Long-lasting kalanchoe flowers come from bright light, a full dry-down between waterings, and prompt deadheading.

Guide 3 · 7 sections

Why Are My Anthurium Flowers Turning Green? Causes

A greening spathe is usually natural ageing, sometimes paired with too much direct light or low light during development.

Guide 4 · 7 sections

How to Grow African Violets: Complete Indoor Care Guide

African violets need bright indirect light, bottom watering, and water that never sits on the fuzzy leaves.

Guide 5 · 7 sections

How to Make Jasmine Bloom Indoors: Care and Tips

Indoor jasmine needs much brighter light than most vines, plus a cooler night period and restraint with nitrogen.

Guide 6 · 7 sections

How to Care for Peace Lily Flowers: Keep Them White Longer

Longevity comes from stable moisture, bright indirect light, moderate humidity and no direct sun on the spathe.

Guide 7 · 7 sections

Best Fertilizer for Indoor Flowering Plants

Use a dilute balanced feed during active growth, and match the plant's substrate; no fertiliser can replace adequate light.

Guide 8 · 7 sections

Why Is My Begonia Dropping Flowers? Common Reasons

Bud drop is usually a response to a sudden change — watering, light, temperature or a move — rather than to a nutrient lack.

Guide 9 · 7 sections

How to Grow Clivia Indoors: Flowering and Care Tips

Clivia needs a cool, dry winter rest and bright indirect light to initiate a flower stalk.

Guide 10 · 7 sections

How to Care for Bromeliad Flowers: After Bloom Guide

The mother rosette flowers once; remove the spent bloom and raise the offsets (pups) it produces.
