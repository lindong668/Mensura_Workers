How to Make Snake Plant Grow Faster: Pro Care Tips

Published 2026-10-30 10:11 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

A sansevieria that has not produced a new leaf in a year looks healthy but refuses to grow, and more water has not helped. Snake plant growth is limited by light and pot-bound roots far more often than by water or fertiliser. Increase brightness first. The sequence matters here: diagnose first, then act once, then leave the plant alone long enough to respond.

Why This Works

Sansevieria uses CAM photosynthesis, opening its stomata at night to limit water loss. It is exceptionally efficient at surviving, not at growing quickly. Its rate is set by total daily light and by how much rhizome room it has; water and nutrients only matter once those two are adequate.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on snake plant: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it. A method that only works in someone else’s room usually skipped No new spear has emerged from the soil line.

What to Avoid

Four responses undo the work above; Do not force growth with high-nitrogen fertiliser is the one people reach for first. Avoid these while No new spear has emerged from the soil line is still present, not only after it clears.

  • Do not force growth with high-nitrogen fertiliser.
  • Do not repot three sizes up "to give it room".
  • Do not bury the leaf bases when repotting.
  • Do not keep it in a windowless corner and expect feed to compensate.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep snake plant from drifting back into the same situation. Fold them into the watering round so A bright window with indirect gets checked at the same time.

  • Rotate the pot so new spears develop evenly.
  • Dust the rigid leaves so they keep photosynthesising.
  • Keep it away from cold window glass in winter.
  • Expect growth only in the warm bright months, and do not panic in winter.

FAQs

How fast should a snake plant grow?

In good indirect light it may add two to four new leaves a season. In a dim room, one or none is normal.

Will fertiliser make it grow faster?

Only if light is already adequate. Otherwise it produces weak growth and salt buildup in the mix.

Should I cut the tallest leaves to encourage new ones?

No. Cut leaves never regrow from the cut edge; new growth only comes from the rhizome at soil level.

Is a bigger pot better?

One size up, yes. A large pot of wet mix around a small root ball is the commonest cause of rot.

Final Thoughts

Give it more light and one modest pot up; stop trying to speed it up with water or feed.
