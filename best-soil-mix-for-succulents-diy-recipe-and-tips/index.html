Best Soil Mix for Succulents: DIY Recipe and Tips

Published 2026-11-05 17:03 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

Standard potting compost stays wet for a week after watering and your succulents keep declining in it. Use a mostly mineral mix that drains within seconds: roughly two parts grit to one part organic potting mix. The sequence matters here: diagnose first, then act once, then leave the plant alone long enough to respond.

What You’ll Need

For succulent soil, the useful kit is short and ordinary:

  • Coarse sand or poultry grit — to keep the root zone airy and free-draining
  • Pumice, perlite or lava rock — to keep the root zone airy and free-draining
  • A small amount of plain potting mix — to keep the root zone airy and free-draining
  • A pot with an oversized drainage hole — so water can drain freely and never sit under the roots
  • A scoop and a bucket for blending — so water can drain freely and never sit under the roots

Safety note. Wear a mask when handling dry perlite or fine grit dust; avoid inhaling mineral dust. Work on a stable surface, and keep pets and children away from cut material.

They separate a simple succulent soil problem from one that needs a different response entirely:

  • Water runs through freely and the surface looks dry within a day.
  • The mix does not compact into a dense wet mass.
  • Roots stay firm and pale rather than dark and thin.

Step-by-Step Guide

Work through the steps in order; each one builds on the previous, and you can stop once the plant is recovering.

  1. Step 1 — Start with two parts mineral grit to one part plain potting mix by volume

    The mineral fraction creates the air pockets that succulent roots need between waterings.

  2. Step 2 — Sieve out fine dust and the smallest particles

    Fines settle into the gaps and turn a fast mix back into a water-holding one.

  3. Step 3 — Wet a sample and time how fast it drains; it should run through within seconds

    A simple drainage test tells you more than any bag label.

  4. Step 4 — Fill the pot without pressing the mix down hard

    Compaction squeezes out the air spaces you just built.

  5. Step 5 — Top-dress with coarse grit to keep the crown dry and reduce fungus gnats

    A dry mineral surface breaks the moist organic layer those flies need.

  6. Step 6 — Repot only when the mix has broken down or stays damp, not on a calendar

    Organic matter decomposes over years, gradually losing drainage.

Why This Works

Succulent roots are adapted to coarse, rapidly drying substrates where a short pulse of rain is followed by air-filled pores. Drainage is governed by particle size distribution: a uniform fine mix holds water by capillary action, while a coarse mineral skeleton leaves macropores that drain by gravity. That physical structure, not the nutrient content, is what prevents rot.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on succulent soil: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it.

What to Avoid

Each item below reverses one of the steps you just read.

  • Do not use pure garden soil or dense peat-only compost.
  • Do not add water-retaining crystals.
  • Do not make the mix richer "to feed" the plant.
  • Do not pack it firmly around the stem.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep succulent soil from drifting back into the same situation.

  • Store the dry mix in a sealed tub so it does not absorb humidity.
  • Match particle size roughly to pot size.
  • Use a shallow pot for small rosettes.
  • Feed separately at half dose in active growth, not by enriching the soil.

FAQs

Can I just use cactus compost from a shop?

Often it is still too peat-heavy. Adding extra pumice or grit usually improves it noticeably.

Is perlite or pumice better?

Both work. Pumice is heavier and stays put; perlite is light and can float to the surface.

Should the mix contain fertiliser?

A small amount is fine, but a mineral, fast-draining structure matters far more than nutrients.

How do I know the mix has failed?

It stays damp for many days, compacts, or smells sour — repot into fresh grit.

Final Thoughts

Two parts coarse mineral grit to one part plain mix, sieved, loosely filled, and tested for fast drainage.

Where this guide applies

This applies to a plant in ordinary indoor potting mix.
