Fuel Economy Converter

Convert fuel consumption between L/100 km, km/L, MPG (US gallon) and MPG (Imperial gallon) — with the gallon distinction handled correctly and the non-linearity explained.

US MPG and UK MPG are different units

A 20% trap. The US gallon is 3.785 L; the Imperial gallon is 4.546 L. The same car, burning identical fuel over an identical distance, scores a higher MPG number in the UK purely because the gallon is bigger. A car rated 30 MPG (US) is about 36 MPG (UK). Comparing the two numbers directly is meaningless.

Why MPG is not linear — and why that matters

MPG is a reciprocal, so equal steps in MPG do not mean equal fuel saved. Going from 20 to 30 MPG saves far more fuel than going from 40 to 50 MPG, even though both are "10 MPG better":

ChangeFuel per 100 km (before)AfterSaved
20 → 30 MPG (US)11.76 L7.84 L3.92 L
30 → 40 MPG (US)7.84 L5.88 L1.96 L
40 → 50 MPG (US)5.88 L4.70 L1.18 L

This is why L/100 km is the better unit for comparisons: it is linear in fuel, so a 1 L/100 km improvement always saves the same amount of fuel regardless of baseline. Several countries adopted it for exactly this reason.

The conversion constants

  • MPG (US) = 235.214583 ÷ (L/100 km)
  • MPG (UK) = 282.480936 ÷ (L/100 km)
  • L/100 km = 235.214583 ÷ MPG (US)

The constants come from 100 km expressed in miles divided by the gallon in litres — which is why they look so arbitrary.

Real-world figures differ from the sticker

Official test-cycle figures are produced under controlled conditions that rarely match actual driving. Cold starts, short trips, air-conditioning load, roof racks, tyre pressure and speed all move real consumption — typically upward. Treat converted figures as a consistent way to compare vehicles, not as a prediction of your fuel bill.
