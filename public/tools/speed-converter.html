Speed Converter

Convert speed across m/s, km/h, mph, ft/s, knots and Mach. Metric and imperial road units convert exactly; Mach does not, and the reason matters.

Mach is not a constant

Mach 1 is not one speed. Mach number is the ratio of your speed to the local speed of sound, and the speed of sound depends on air temperature — roughly 331 m/s at 0 °C and about 343 m/s at 20 °C. At altitude, where air is colder, it drops further. This tool uses the standard sea-level value of 340.3 m/s, which is a convention for comparison, not a physical constant.

Knots: speed as an angle

One knot is one nautical mile per hour, and a nautical mile was defined as one minute of latitude. So a knot is literally "minutes of latitude per hour" — which is why a navigator can measure distance by comparing against the latitude scale printed on the chart, with no separate conversion. That convenience is the entire reason the unit survives in aviation and maritime use.

Quick mental conversions

  • mph → km/h: multiply by 1.6 (exact is 1.609344).
  • km/h → mph: multiply by 0.62.
  • m/s → km/h: multiply by 3.6 — this one is exact.
  • km/h → m/s: divide by 3.6, or halve then subtract 10% for a rough figure.

Choosing the unit that fits the context

Speed units are not interchangeable in practice because each one was built for a specific job. Knots exist so a navigator can read distance straight off a chart. Mach exists because aircraft performance depends on how close you are to the speed of sound, not on any fixed number. km/h and mph are road units, chosen by whichever side of the imperial divide a country landed on.

Converting between them is exact for the road units and conventional for Mach, but the number alone rarely carries the meaning: 500 knots means something specific to a pilot that 926 km/h does not. Convert when you need to compare against a limit or a specification written in another unit, and keep the original unit when the context depends on it.
