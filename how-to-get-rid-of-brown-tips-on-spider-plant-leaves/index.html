How to Get Rid of Brown Tips on Spider Plant Leaves

Published 2026-10-16 15:03 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

The long arching leaves look healthy except for dry brown ends that keep coming back after you trim them. Brown tips on chlorophytum usually come from dissolved salts, irregular watering or very dry air — not from a disease. Work in this order; each step depends on the one before it, and skipping ahead usually means undoing it later.

If Browning is confined to the very tips and sometimes is absent, use the related guides at the foot of this page instead. They separate a simple spider plant problem from one that needs a different response entirely:

  • Browning is confined to the very tips and sometimes the leaf margins.
  • New leaves emerge clean and brown later.
  • The pot has been watered with small frequent top-ups.

Why This Works

Chlorophytum moves water to the leaf tip, where evaporation leaves behind whatever was dissolved in it. Over time soluble salts accumulate at that endpoint and kill the terminal cells. The pattern is therefore a record of water quality and watering habit, not an infection — which is why spraying or feeding makes it worse.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on spider plant: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it. Once you see Soak the pot thoroughly until water runs this way, the rest of the guide reads as one argument.

What to Avoid

The list is short because Do not cut the whole leaf back and its close variants cover most relapses. Each item below reverses one of the steps you just read.

  • Do not cut the whole leaf back to the base.
  • Do not add fertiliser to "fix" brown tips.
  • Do not mist repeatedly in place of flushing the soil.
  • Do not assume it is a fungus and reach for a spray.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep spider plant from drifting back into the same situation. These small routines stop How to Get Rid of Brown coming back without adding products.

  • Use rainwater or filtered water if tap water is hard.
  • Flush the pot every four to six weeks.
  • Keep it in bright indirect light, out of midday sun.
  • Accept that a few old tips will brown on a long-lived plant.

FAQs

Will the brown tips turn green again?

No. Trim the dead tissue and judge the fix by whether new leaves stay clean at the tip.

Is fluoride in tap water really a problem?

Spider plants are among the more fluoride-sensitive common houseplants; persistent tip burn with good watering is a classic sign.

Should I increase humidity?

Very dry air can contribute, but flushing salts and correcting the watering pattern does more than a humidity tray.

Are brown tips a sign of root rot?

Usually not. Root rot shows soft yellow lower leaves and a sour mix, not crisp dry tips on otherwise firm leaves.

Final Thoughts

Flush the pot, soften the water, cut only dead tissue, and let the next leaves prove it worked.
