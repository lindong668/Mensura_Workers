How to Kill Spider Mites on Indoor Plants: Complete Guide

Published 2026-09-22 19:26 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

Fine stippling appears on leaves with barely visible webbing in the axils, and the plant looks dusty and tired. Spider mites thrive in hot dry air; rinse the foliage, raise humidity, and repeat a contact treatment every few days. Give each step time to show its effect before adding another intervention, otherwise you cannot tell which one helped.

Why This Works

Spider mites are not insects but tiny arachnids that pierce individual leaf cells, leaving the characteristic fine stipple. They protect colonies with silk webbing and reproduce very fast in warm, low-humidity conditions — exactly the environment created by a sunny window over a radiator. Their eggs are relatively tolerant of contact treatments, which is why the calendar of repeat applications matters more than the product chosen.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on spider mites: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it. Diagnose first, act once, then leave A sink or shower with alone long enough to answer.

What to Avoid

Each item below reverses one of the steps you just read. Four responses undo the work above; Do not use a strong jet that is the one people reach for first.

  • Do not use a strong jet that shreds soft leaves.
  • Do not assume dry brown tips are mites without checking the paper test.
  • Do not fertilise an actively infested plant.
  • Do not put an oiled plant straight into direct sun.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep spider mites from drifting back into the same situation. Fold them into the watering round so A sink or shower with gets checked at the same time.

  • Inspect the newest growth weekly during heated winter months.
  • Group humidity-loving plants together.
  • Wipe dust off leaves; dusty surfaces favour mites.
  • Quarantine a new plant before placing it next to a Calathea or a ficus.

FAQs

How do I know it is spider mites and not dry air?

Tap a leaf over white paper. Moving specks confirm mites; plain stippling without movement points to water or heat stress.

Why do they keep returning?

Eggs survive the first treatment and hatch days later. Repeat on a short cycle.

Does humidity actually help?

Yes. Mites develop fastest in hot dry conditions, so a stable humid microclimate slows the population.

Can I just rinse with water?

Rinsing helps a lot but rarely clears eggs on its own; combine with repeat contact treatment.

Final Thoughts

Paper-test first, rinse thoroughly, treat undersides on a three-to-five-day cycle, and fix the dry heat.
