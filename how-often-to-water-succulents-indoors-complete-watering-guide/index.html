How Often to Water Succulents Indoors: Complete Watering Guide

Published 2026-11-09 14:19 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

You are never sure whether a succulent is thirsty or still full, and a fixed schedule has already killed one. Water succulents only after the mix is completely dry, then soak and drain. Indoors that is typically every ten to twenty days, less in winter. The sequence matters here: diagnose first, then act once, then leave the plant alone long enough to respond.

Why This Works

Succulents store water in leaves and stems and use CAM photosynthesis, opening stomata at night. They are built for a pulse of water followed by a long dry period, not for constant availability. In a home, low light and cool temperatures slow their water use dramatically, which is why a schedule that works in June rots the same plant in December.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on indoor succulents: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it. Diagnose first, act once, then leave A gritty fast-draining mix — alone long enough to answer.

  • Err on the side of one more dry day, never one less.

FAQs

Is every two weeks right?

It is a reasonable indoor starting point in bright light. In a dim winter it may be four weeks or more.

Should I mist my succulents?

No. Misting wets the leaf surface without reaching the roots and can encourage rot in rosette centres.

How do I tell thirsty from overwatered?

Thirsty: firm, slightly wrinkled, dry mix. Overwatered: translucent, mushy, yellow lower leaves in damp mix.

Do I water right after repotting?

Wait a few days so damaged roots can seal before the next soak.

Final Thoughts

Soak only after a confirmed full dry-down, drain completely, and let the plant set the interval.
