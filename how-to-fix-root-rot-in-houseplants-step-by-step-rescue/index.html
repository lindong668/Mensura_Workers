How to Fix Root Rot in Houseplants: Step-by-Step Rescue

Published 2026-10-28 14:03 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

A plant wilts in wet soil, the lower leaves yellow, and the pot smells sour when you lift it. Unpot, cut all soft dark roots back to firm tissue, repot into a fresh airy mix, and hold back on water. Give each step time to show its effect before adding another intervention, otherwise you cannot tell which one helped.

What You’ll Need

For root rot, the useful kit is short and ordinary:

  • Clean sharp pruners — for a clean cut that does not tear plant tissue
  • A fresh free-draining mix — to keep the root zone airy and free-draining
  • A clean pot with large drainage holes — so water can drain freely and never sit under the roots
  • Gloves — to protect your hands from damp tissue, sap or spines
  • A dry shaded recovery spot — to give the plant the right spot and conditions while it recovers

Safety note. Wear gloves when handling rotted tissue; disinfect tools afterwards and keep debris away from pets. Work on a stable surface, and keep pets and children away from cut material.

They separate a simple root rot problem from one that needs a different response entirely:

  • Healthy roots are firm and tan; rotted ones are dark, slimy and slip off their core.
  • The mix stays wet long after watering and smells sour.
  • The stem base may be darkening.

Step-by-Step Guide

Work through the steps in order; each one builds on the previous, and you can stop once the plant is recovering.

  1. Step 1 — Unpot and gently wash or tease the mix away so you can see the whole root ball

    You cannot judge rot from the leaves; diagnosis happens in the hand.

  2. Step 2 — Cut every soft, dark, hollow root back to firm pale tissue, sterilising the blade as you go

    Any mushy tissue left behind continues to break down and spread upward.

  3. Step 3 — If the stem base is soft, cut above it and treat the healthy top as a cutting

    Once rot reaches the crown, the original lower stem cannot be saved.

  4. Step 4 — Discard the old mix and wash the pot before reuse

    The old substrate holds both the moisture and the organisms that caused the problem.

  5. Step 5 — Repot at the same depth in a fresh airy mix and do not water for several days

    Damaged roots need oxygen first; a light first watering comes later.

  6. Step 6 — Keep it in bright indirect light, out of direct sun, and rebuild a skewer-based watering routine

    Recovery is visible as a new bud, not as old leaves re-greening.

Why This Works

Roots require oxygen for active uptake. In a saturated, fine-textured mix, air-filled pores disappear and root cells die; opportunistic organisms then break down the dead tissue, producing the sour smell and the slimy texture. Once the root mass is reduced, the plant cannot supply its leaves even though water is abundant — the counterintuitive wilt. The cure is structural: remove dead tissue, restore air-filled porosity and let the surviving roots re-establish before regular watering returns.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on root rot: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it.

What to Avoid

Each item below reverses one of the steps you just read.

  • Do not water more to "flush" the rot.
  • Do not fertilise a plant with a reduced root system.
  • Do not reuse the soggy original mix.
  • Do not put a freshly repotted plant into full sun to dry it.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep root rot from drifting back into the same situation.

  • Use a skewer before every watering from now on.
  • Choose a pot only as large as the surviving root ball.
  • Keep the crown at exactly its previous depth.
  • Empty the saucer every single time.

FAQs

Can a plant recover from root rot?

Yes if enough firm roots and healthy stem remain. It may take a growing season to look full again.

Why does it wilt when the soil is wet?

Dead roots cannot take up water, so the plant wilts from lack of uptake in the middle of plenty.

Should I water right after repotting?

Wait several days, then one thorough soak and a complete drain.

How do I prevent it recurring?

Coarser mix, real drainage, a full dry-down between waterings, and no standing runoff.

Final Thoughts

Cut to firm tissue, repot in fresh airy mix, wait several days before watering, and judge recovery by new buds.

Where this guide applies

This applies to a plant in ordinary indoor potting mix.
