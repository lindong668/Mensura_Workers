How to Grow Plants From Cuttings in Soil: General Guide

Published 2026-11-10 20:23 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

Cuttings rooted in water keep failing when you move them into a pot, and you want a general soil method. Use a loose, barely moist medium, keep humidity around the leaf, and avoid both stagnant water and a full dry-down. Start with the simplest check, because the most obvious-looking cause is often not the one at work.

Why This Works

Adventitious roots form at the cut end from cambium or neighbouring parenchyma, a process that needs both moisture and oxygen. A saturated medium fills the air pores and the wound rots; a dry one desiccates the unrooted stem before it can supply the leaf. Meanwhile the cutting still has leaves losing water but no roots to replace it, so moderate ambient humidity reduces that deficit until new roots take over. This balance, not any rooting powder, is the core of the method.

What to Avoid

  • Do not keep propagation mix saturated.
  • Do not seal cuttings in an unvented wet container.
  • Do not fertilise an unrooted cutting.
  • Do not pull cuttings up repeatedly to "check" roots.

  • Label the date and the parent plant.

  • Keep them in bright indirect light, not direct sun.
  • Harden off gradually by opening the vent over a week before normal care.

FAQs

Should I root in water or soil?

Soil-rooted cuttings avoid the difficult water-to-soil transition. Water is easier to observe but the roots must adapt later.

How wet should the mix be?

Barely moist and airy — it should crumble when squeezed, not release water.

Do I need a humidity dome?

Moderate humidity with ventilation helps soft cuttings; a sealed wet box causes rot.

When can I feed?

Only after you see new top growth, which confirms the roots are working.

Final Thoughts

Loose barely-moist medium, a shallow insert, moderate vented humidity, and no feeding until new growth.
