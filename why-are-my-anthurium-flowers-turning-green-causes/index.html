Why Are My Anthurium Flowers Turning Green? Causes

Published 2026-11-03 11:03 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

The red or pink spathe on an anthurium has faded to green while the plant otherwise looks healthy. A greening spathe is usually natural ageing, sometimes paired with too much direct light or low light during development. The sequence matters here: diagnose first, then act once, then leave the plant alone long enough to respond.

Why This Works

The anthurium "flower" is a spathe — a modified leaf — surrounding a spadix of tiny true flowers. Its red, pink or white comes from pigments laid down as it develops; as the spathe ages, those pigments degrade while chlorophyll persists, shifting it toward green. Light during development sets how much pigment is deposited, so both too little and harsh direct light affect colour, but in an old bloom the change is simply senescence.

FAQs

Is a green anthurium flower normal?

Yes, on an older spathe. Pigment fades as the modified leaf ages.

Can it turn red again?

No. That individual spathe will not regain colour; the next one is the test.

Why are new flowers pale?

Usually insufficient light during development, or root stress from a dense wet mix.

Should I cut it off?

Once fully green, yes. Cut at the base so the plant puts energy into the next bloom.

Final Thoughts

Judge the newest spathe: if it is fully coloured, the plant is healthy and the green one is just old.
