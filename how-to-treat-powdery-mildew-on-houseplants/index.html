How to Treat Powdery Mildew on Houseplants

Published 2026-09-10 22:31 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

A white powdery film appears on leaves and stems, looking like flour dust that does not wash off with plain water. Powdery mildew is favoured by poor airflow and humid still air with dry leaf surfaces; improve airflow and remove infected tissue. Start with the simplest check, because the most obvious-looking cause is often not the one at work.

Why This Works

Powdery mildew fungi grow on the leaf surface, sending haustoria into epidermal cells. Unlike many fungi, they do not need free water on the leaf — high relative humidity, still air and moderate temperatures are enough, which is why a crowded indoor windowsill is ideal. Spores move on the slightest air current, so isolation and spacing are part of the treatment rather than optional extras.

That is also why the common "quick fix" tends to fail on powdery mildew: it treats the visible symptom rather than the condition producing it. A method that only works in someone else’s room usually skipped White patches spread as a flat powder rather than.

What to Avoid

The list is short because Do not mist an infected plant in and its close variants cover most relapses. Each item below reverses one of the steps you just read.

  • Do not mist an infected plant in the evening.
  • Do not crowd it back into the group before it is clear.
  • Do not rely on a single wipe while airflow stays poor.
  • Do not fertilise heavily to "outgrow" the mildew.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep powdery mildew from drifting back into the same situation. Fold them into the watering round so Clean snips — kept clean gets checked at the same time.

  • Give each pot enough space for air to move.
  • Water at soil level in the morning.
  • Check the underside of leaves during routine watering.
  • Quarantine new plants for two weeks.

FAQs

Is powdery mildew the same as mealybug wax?

No. Mildew is a flat powdery fungal film; mealybugs are discrete cottony clusters you can lift off.

Will the white patches disappear?

The existing marks generally do not. Judge success by clean new growth.

Does humidity cause it?

High humidity with still air and dry leaf surfaces favours it. Air movement is the practical fix indoors.

Can I just wash it off?

Wiping reduces spores, but without airflow change and repeat treatment it usually returns.

Final Thoughts

Remove infected leaves, improve gentle airflow, treat both surfaces, and judge recovery by clean new growth.
