How to Get an Orchid to Rebloom: Step-by-Step Guide

Published 2026-10-07 13:31 · Elena Marsh · Photo sources

A phalaenopsis finished flowering months ago and has produced only leaves ever since. Reblooming is triggered by a sustained modest temperature drop plus bright indirect light; feeding is secondary. Start with the simplest check, because the most obvious-looking cause is often not the one at work.

Why This Works

Phalaenopsis is a monopodial epiphyte from warm, humid forests with modest seasonal temperature shifts. Its thick leaves and aerial-velamen roots store both water and energy. Flower initiation is cued mainly by a sustained night temperature drop, but the plant must first have a positive energy balance — which depends on bright diffuse light and functioning roots. Feed cannot substitute for either.

What to Avoid

The list is short because Do not cut a still-green old spike and its close variants cover most relapses. Each item below reverses one of the steps you just read.

  • Do not cut a still-green old spike unless it has clearly browned.
  • Do not force bloom with high-phosphorus feed on a weak plant.
  • Do not place it in direct sun to "warm" it.
  • Do not repot while a spike is forming.

Daily Care Tips

Once the immediate problem is handled, these small habits keep phalaenopsis orchid from drifting back into the same situation. These small routines stop How to Get an Orchid to coming back without adding products.

  • Clean the leaves so the thick blades keep working.
  • Keep it away from heater airflow and cold glass.
  • Note when the spike first appeared so you can repeat the trigger next year.
  • After flowering, cut the spike above a dormant node only if the plant is strong.

FAQs

How long until an orchid reblooms?

Typically once or twice a year, with a spike appearing a few weeks after the cooler-night period.

Should I cut the old flower spike?

If fully brown, yes at the base. A green spike may branch and flower again from a node.

Does it need a dark period like a Christmas cactus?

No. Phalaenopsis responds primarily to a day-night temperature difference, not to night length.

Why are the leaves wrinkled instead?

That points to root loss or underwatering. Fix the root zone before trying to trigger flowering.

Final Thoughts

Healthy roots, bright indirect light, and a consistent cooler-night period for two to three weeks.
